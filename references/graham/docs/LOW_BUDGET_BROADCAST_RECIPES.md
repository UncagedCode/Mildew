# Low-Budget Broadcast Recipes Using Current Assets

These recipes demonstrate how the existing asset set can already cover common Mildew situations.

## 1. Normal question introduction

1. `restrained_smile` — 0.8 sec.
2. Dynamic/preauthored line begins.
3. `presenting` / speech-cycle — 0.5–0.9 sec.
4. Cut to game title/question graphic while voice finishes.
5. Return to `reading_cards` or `restrained_smile` after submissions begin.

No new video required.

## 2. Correct answer reveal

1. Question graphic reveals answer.
2. Audience sting/applause.
3. Cut to `presenter_smile` for 0.8–1.5 sec.
4. Graham congratulation audio.
5. Cut to scoreboard/player podium before dynamic player name/score if lip-sync would be obvious.

## 3. Player gives a ridiculous wrong answer

1. Answer appears on TV.
2. Hold silence 0.25–0.6 sec.
3. Cut to `neutral`.
4. Graham deadpan line.
5. Optional audience laugh.
6. Cut back to game.

This is cheaper and funnier than requiring a unique reaction video.

## 4. Graham humiliates a player

1. Cut to `neutral` or `return_tense`.
2. Graham sets up special question.
3. Cut to player's name/podium and giant humiliating title graphic.
4. Audio continues off-screen.
5. Return to `restrained_smile` or `presenter_smile` only if the joke requires forced cheerfulness.

## 5. Backstage bang incident

Best current asset use:

1. Normal `presenting` state.
2. Play backstage bang.
3. Audience ambience cuts.
4. Use `reference/motion/irritated_turn_hold.mp4` as reference/runtime sequence if performance permits, OR bridge through a quick camera cut into `irritated_hold`.
5. Off-camera “Keep going.”
6. Use recovery motion sequence or cut to another shot.
7. Return on `return_tense`.
8. Begin “Right…” before switching to `recovered_presenter`.
9. Immediately continue normal game.

## 6. Reconnect wait

1. `reading_cards` while WAITING FOR PLAYER graphic appears.
2. After several seconds, cut to `neutral`.
3. Graham comments.
4. Return to connection graphic.
5. If player returns, use `restrained_smile` / audience applause.
6. If they do not return, use `neutral` or `irritated_hold` briefly, then continue.

Do not attempt to animate Graham for the entire 30-second wait.

## 7. Graham staring at camera

1. `neutral`.
2. Remove almost all camera motion.
3. Remove/reduce audience bed.
4. Hold longer than normal: perhaps 4–10 sec only for a rare incident.
5. Tiny analogue movement continues.
6. Cut directly into a bright game sting or advert.

## 8. Commercial break/off-air tease without new FMV

Until an off-air Graham asset exists:

1. Fake advert finishes.
2. Accidental half-second return to `reading_cards`.
3. Graham audio/crew mutter off-screen.
4. Cut to black/test card/next advert.

Do not fake a complex exhausted performance with the wrong still.

## 9. Dynamic name line

Line: “Aaron has taken the lead with eight thousand four hundred points.”

1. Graham visible for “Aaron has taken the lead…” if desired.
2. Cut to Aaron podium immediately.
3. Show 8,400 points.
4. Dynamic speech finishes over graphic.
5. Return to Graham reaction.

This is normal television direction and eliminates the need for perfect dynamic lip-sync.

## 10. Graham becomes angry

Until a dedicated angry state exists:

1. Use `irritated_hold` only briefly.
2. Let voice and silence do most of the work.
3. Do not repeatedly alternate between neutral/irritated frames.
4. Cut away after the angry line.
5. Return later on `return_tense` or `reading_cards`, not immediately on a giant smile.
