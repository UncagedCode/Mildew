# 05 — Scoring, Rot and Session Flow

## Scoring philosophy

Mildew is competitive, but not an esport. Exact scores matter enough to create winners, close races, streaks and reversals. The Director/Graham care heavily about **relative position and behaviour**, not only mathematical optimisation.

Use a broadly consistent scoring scale so totals feel coherent across games.

Typical standard question/action value: **500–1,500 points**.

Finale: roughly **1.5× normal scoring**, not a 3× wipeout that makes earlier play irrelevant.

## Speed

Speed is a small secondary reward.

Target principle:

- ~85–90% of possible value is accuracy/quality;
- ~10–15% can come from speed where appropriate.

Players should not win trivia by mashing.

## Wrong answers

Normally:

- wrong = 0;
- no routine negative scoring.

Specific authored risk/wager variants may create penalties or lost wagers.

## Streaks

Correct streaks receive small bonuses and create presentation/callback opportunities. Avoid runaway multipliers.

Wrong-answer streaks create Graham/punishment opportunities rather than large mathematical penalties.

## Ties

Usually resolve a final tie with a short ridiculous sudden-death format appropriate to the episode. It can be a mini-question or tiny bespoke tiebreaker.

Very rarely Graham may simply express a preference or announce his own social winner, but the official record should preserve the actual tie unless a clearly authored special segment changes it.

## Rot

Rot is separate from points.

It measures how questionable Mildew considers the player's in-game behaviour/knowledge, not moral worth.

Rot may increase for:

- quickly recognising disgusting material;
- choosing vile options;
- strong performance in gross categories;
- disturbing written answers;
- suspicious button choices;
- selected game-specific behaviours.

Rot can rise even when a player is correct.

Rot does **not decrease during a session**.

Track:

- Tonight's Rot;
- Lifetime Rot (persistent).

Lifetime milestones may receive Graham callbacks, but do not turn Rot into an upgrade currency.

## Score display

Scores are mostly shown between games/rounds, not as a permanent HUD.

Graham may delay a scoreboard:

> “Let's see the scores.”
>
> nothing
>
> “Apparently not.”

Rare scoreboard anomalies may include:

- phantom contestant at zero;
- previous-session score briefly visible;
- impossible negative number that corrects itself;
- name ordering glitch;
- a score changing back.

Keep rare so the official score remains generally trustworthy.

## Personal end awards

End-of-show awards are a major payoff and should be highly personalised.

Examples:

- Fastest Genital Identification
- Most Confidently Wrong
- Most Likely to Touch It
- Best Artist / Worst Artist
- Most Rotten
- Surprisingly Normal
- Animal Expert
- Concerningly Knowledgeable About Parasites
- Graham's Favourite
- Graham's Least Favourite
- Horse Again
- Quietest Contestant
- Most Helpful During Do Not Press That
- Destroyer of Studio Power

Graham's Favourite/Least Favourite are **not guaranteed**. He may say:

> “I didn't care for any of you.”

Awards usually do not affect future sessions, but selected titles can persist for callbacks.

A returning player might briefly enter as:

> AARON
>
> FORMER GENITAL EXPERT

## Fake prizes

The winner receives a terrible fictional prize with exaggerated enthusiasm.

Examples:

- commemorative ceramic badger;
- fax machine;
- 47 pounds of ham;
- microwave from the 1980s;
- weekend in Scunthorpe, transport not included;
- year's supply of mayonnaise;
- terrible old hatchback;
- contents of Locker 14.

Selected prizes may persist as visual props or later dialogue:

> “Welcome back, Aaron. How was the ham?”

Do not make prize persistence predictable.

## Standard 45-minute session

Approximate structure, not a rigid timeline:

1. Opening titles — ~30–60 sec
2. Lobby-to-show contestant introduction — ~1–2 min
3. Game 1 — ~5–7 min
4. Advert/interstitial — ~30–60 sec
5. Game 2 — ~5–8 min
6. Viewer Poll / Graham Fact / production beat — ~30–90 sec
7. Game 3 — ~6–9 min
8. Midpoint commercial/bathroom break — ~90 sec, resumable early if all ready
9. Game 4 — ~6–9 min
10. Optional punishment/interruption — variable
11. Finale — ~7–10 min, ~1.5× scoring
12. Awards/prize/credits — ~2–3 min

Usually around **five core games** in a standard episode, depending on actual game durations.

## Midpoint commercial break

Purpose:

- adverts;
- natural bathroom/snack window;
- give the broadcast breathing room;
- a strong opportunity for rare apparently off-air weirdness.

Phones show a clear return state. If every connected player marks ready, resume early.

Most breaks are normal. Rarely:

- Graham believes camera is off;
- he loosens tie / looks exhausted;
- crew speak to him;
- he notices the tally light;
- abrupt cut to advert.

Do not make every break scary.

## Manual pause

Always available through TV remote/system UI.

Theme:

> TRANSMISSION PAUSED

Options:

- Resume
- Broadcast Settings
- End Transmission

Pausing is a real usability function and is never blocked as a joke.

## Runtime overrun

Mildew may intentionally run ~2–5 minutes long.

Example:

> ANNOUNCER: “We're out of time.”
>
> GRAHAM: “No.”

The Director may schedule one final short segment.

## Programme schedule

Occasionally show a fake schedule graphic. It may be incomplete, wrong, changed or contain a segment that never appears. Players normally do not know the next game.
